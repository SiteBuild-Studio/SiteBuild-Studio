Ask This Before You Build Anything

The one question every small business should be asked before a website ever gets built — and why guessing at the answer costs more than asking.

6 min read

Most web designers make the same mistake with small businesses. They either say no, because the project looks too small to bother with, or they say yes and hand over a template that could belong to any business in any city. Neither actually helps. What small businesses need isn't a bigger sales pitch — it's a few honest questions before anything gets built.

The question that changes everything

Before wireframes, before colors, before a single line of code, there's one question worth asking: are you planning to stay exactly where you are, or are you building toward more locations — more branches, maybe even franchising out one day?

It sounds like a small thing to ask. It isn't. A business that's staying put needs a site built entirely around one address — one Google Business listing, one set of local keywords, content written for one neighborhood's worth of customers. A business that's planning to grow into two, five, or twenty locations needs something structurally different from day one: a site that can add a "Locations" page without a rebuild, schema markup that can describe more than one address, and — if the growth ever crosses a border — a domain strategy that accounts for that early, rather than scrambling for it later.

Take a laundromat as an example. A single laundromat is a straightforward local business — the whole job is getting found by people within a few kilometers who need a machine free right now. But a laundromat that's grown into several branches across a city, with more planned, is a completely different business wearing the same name. It needs every branch findable in its own area, one consistent brand across all of them, and a site built so that adding branch six doesn't mean redoing branches one through five. Nobody builds that by accident. It only happens if someone asked the growth question first.

A few more questions worth asking before assuming anything

Growth intent is the big one, but it's not the only assumption worth checking before a build starts:

  • Are you the face of this business, or are you building something that could one day be handed over or sold?
  • Are your customers walking past your door, or could they realistically be finding you from anywhere?
  • Do you want people coming to you, or are you doing most of the outreach yourself right now?

Every one of those answers changes the build. A business built around its owner's personal reputation needs a site that puts a face and a story front and center. A business built to run without them needs the opposite — systems and content that don't depend on any one person being there to explain it. None of that is guesswork if someone just asks.

Why being reachable actually matters

Someone reached out to us directly not long ago — a startup that needed serious, ongoing SEO work and simply looked for a way to get in touch. That conversation only happened because there was a way to actually reach us. It's easy to underestimate how often the right opportunity starts with someone just being able to send a message.

But there's a balance to strike. Splashing a contact email across the top of every page invites noise along with the real enquiries — quick questions, wrong-fit requests, the occasional bit of spam. Putting the way to reach us at the very end of something like this article, after someone has actually read the whole thing, tends to filter for people who are genuinely serious about the conversation. It's not about being harder to reach. It's about making sure the people who do reach out mean it.
